Your Apollo filters were built for job titles. Your ICP is a sentence. That gap is where most cold email budgets leak, and AI can now close it.

Your filters return a long list

You know who you sell to. So you open Apollo and set filters: title, industry, company size, country. Apollo hands back a list, sometimes thousands of people. Then three things go wrong.

  • Apollo data is sometimes wrong. A title is two jobs old, an industry tag is a guess. One of our clients sells shockwave devices to chiropractors; the filters also returned hospitalists and a urology physician.
  • Filters can't say what a company does. "Clinics that offer elective treatments" or "startups that ship AI hardware" fit in no dropdown.
  • Filters miss the signals that matter. Is the company hiring? Does its website mention the product you complement? No filter reads that.

Jev reads every lead like you would

Jev 1.13 is a model by TypeSafe built to judge, not to write. You give it a piece of text and a question, and it answers yes or no with how sure it is. We use it as the checkpoint between Apollo and your inbox, across our pipes: {{raw volume.rounded}} questions since September 19, 2026.

How it works for you

  1. You write your ICP in one sentence. One client wrote: "practicing chiropractors in the US who decide on or use shockwave therapy devices." That sentence is the brief.
  2. Jev checks every Apollo lead against it, before we pay for anyone. The name, title and company are free to read; the email costs a credit. A chiropractor who owns a clinic: yes, revealed. A hospital doctor the filters let through: no, and no credit is spent. Each verdict is kept, so no lead is checked twice.
  3. You add the signals that make a lead hot. Our AI suggests them for your offer, you pick the ones you want, and Jev reads the evidence (the company website, its job posts, its LinkedIn page): "Does the company website mention an FDA 510(k) clearance?" "Does the company have open job postings for QA engineers?" "Is the company hiring sales development reps?" "Does the homepage mention elective treatments?"
