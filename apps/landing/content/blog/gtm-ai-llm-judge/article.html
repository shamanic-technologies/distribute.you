One client: Apollo filters vs. one sentence

One of our clients is a company that sells to chiropractic clinics. Here are the Apollo filters we set for them and their ICP, side by side, as stored in our database.

The Apollo filters

  • Seniority: Owner, Founder
  • Location: United States
  • Company keywords: chiropractic, chiropractor
  • Similar titles: on

The ICP, in one sentence

"Owners and clinic founders of chiropractic practices in the United States, meaning practicing chiropractors who run their own clinic, under titles such as Owner, Founder, Co-Founder, Practice Owner, Clinic Director or Doctor of Chiropractic (DC) who owns the practice. Also in the target are the people who assist or advise those owners on growing the practice, such as their executive assistants, office or practice managers, chiefs of staff, marketing and growth advisors, business coaches and consultants, and the managers of in-house or external marketing and patient acquisition teams serving them. Out are staff with no stake in marketing, sales or growth, such as billing and insurance coders, front desk reception, clinical support and therapy staff, IT, and legal or compliance, and out are owners outside chiropractic, including other healthcare or medical practices."

The filters say: owners and founders, in the US, at companies Apollo tags with chiropractic. The sentence adds what the filters cannot say: who else matters (the people who advise the owner), and who to leave out, such as "owners outside chiropractic".

Apollo matched 2,502 people to these filters, and Jev has checked the first 330 against the sentence so far. Most were the clinic owners the client wanted. About 1 in 4 were not: their companies match the keyword "chiropractic", but they run an animal hospital, a physical therapy clinic or a billing company.

Skipped before we paid for their email

  • Veterinarian and Owner of an animal hospital
  • Owner of a physical therapy clinic
  • Owner of a medical billing company
  • Owner and massage therapist of a bodywork studio

Kept by Jev

  • Owner and Chiropractor of a chiropractic clinic
  • Founder and Doctor of Chiropractic of a chiropractic clinic

Jev kept 239 clinic owners and skipped 91 people, before we paid for a single email. Only the 239 kept moved on to the paid step, where we buy the email.

What Jev does

Jev 1.13 is a model by TypeSafe built to judge rather than to write. You give it a piece of text and a question; it answers yes or no and says how sure it is. We placed it between Apollo and the moment we pay for an email. Across our clients' campaigns it answered 120k+ questions since September 19, 2026.

  1. You write your ICP in one sentence. In your words, not in dropdown values.
  2. Jev checks each Apollo lead against it. The name, title and company are free to read; the email costs a credit, so the check comes first.
  3. You add the signals that make a lead hot. Our AI suggests some for your offer, you pick the ones you want, and Jev reads the evidence on the company website, its job posts or its LinkedIn page. Real examples from our clients: "Does the company website mention an FDA 510(k) clearance?", "Does the company have open job postings for QA engineers?", and for the client above: "Does the company's homepage mention accepting new patients?"

What it changes

The budget goes to people in the ICP, the ones who can sign up or book a meeting. The check itself costs a fraction of a cent per lead, and our clients report a 4.1x median ROI on what they spend with us.

The questions we get

"What if Jev makes a mistake?" Jev keeps every lead with a 50% chance or more of fitting the ICP, so a lead in doubt still gets the email. We started stricter and lowered the bar on September 29 when we saw good leads just below it.

"Why not tighter Apollo filters?" We use them too, and they come first. But part of an ICP has no dropdown: "a chiropractic clinic, not an animal hospital that also offers chiropractic care", or "a clinic accepting new patients", which lives on the clinic's website.

"Does the check cost extra?" A fraction of a cent per lead, against about $0.10 to buy one email. Each verdict is stored, so the same lead is not checked twice.

"What if my ICP changes?" Rewrite the sentence. The leads are checked again against the new words.

Replies, too

The same idea runs on replies. More than half of the replies to our cold emails are out-of-office messages or auto-replies, in many languages ("Ich geniesse gerade Ferien"). Our reply classifier, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, labels them and Jev double-checks the label, so the replies you read and the numbers you track come mostly from real people.
