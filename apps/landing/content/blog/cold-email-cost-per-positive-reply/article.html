Key takeaway

A positive reply to our cold emails cost $185 on average, measured on what our clients paid:

  • $185 per positive reply, average since April 2026
  • About $4 per website visit, counting human clicks only
  • A measured price, not a rate card: everything spent, divided by every positive reply

The question

Ask what cold email costs and you usually get a reply rate, or a price per lead. Neither tells you what you pay for the thing you want: a prospect who answers with interest.

We run cold email for our clients and bill each campaign for what it spends. So we can answer from the bill. We added up what our clients paid for the emails that ask for a reply, and divided by the positive replies those emails brought back.

The answer

A positive reply cost $185 on average. That is everything spent since April 2026, divided by every positive reply since, across all our clients.

The running average read $182 after April and stands at $185 today. A positive reply is a prospect answering with interest. Auto-replies, out-of-office messages and refusals do not count.

Month by month

Single months swing between $145 in August and $420 in September. One month carries only a handful of positive replies, so one reply more or less moves its bar a long way. Read the average since the start, not the latest month.

What a website visit cost

Some campaigns ask for a click to the client's site rather than a reply. On the emails we send ourselves with a link, the only ones whose clicks we can check for a real person, a website visit cost about $4 on average since April 2026.

A visit costs far less because many more people click than answer. The two are different goals, priced on different emails, so one price does not convert into the other.

How to read your own number

Set the price of a positive reply against what one new customer is worth to you. A positive reply is the start of a conversation, not a sale, so count how many of them it takes you to sign one customer.

Our average spans every client and every offer we ran. Yours will sit above or below it, depending on what you sell and to whom. The point is to see it: a price you measure on your own campaigns, not one you are quoted up front.

What this data cannot tell you

  • Positive replies are rare, so the average carries a range: the true figure most likely sits between $135 and $262 per positive reply (95% interval).
  • It is an average across all our clients. One campaign can cost a lot more or a lot less per positive reply.
  • Only people we started writing to at least 21 days before the read count, so a reply still on its way does not make the price look higher than it is.

Notes

  • Positive replies: 38 from 98,078 cold emails on campaigns that ask for a reply, $185 each, 95% interval $135 to $262.
  • Website visits: 23 from 1,181 cold emails that carry a link, $4.16 each, 95% interval $2.77 to $6.57. Only clicks through our own redirect count, where a human click can be told from a mail security scanner.
  • Emails sent from April 2026, read from our production database on October 3, 2026. People first written to before September 12, 2026 count, with every outcome since. The price is what clients were billed; intervals are Poisson on the outcome count. A measurement, not a split test.
  • The figures come from our internal research page, copied by the scripts on GitHub.
