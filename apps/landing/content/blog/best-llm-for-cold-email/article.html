Key takeaway

For a cold email that asks for a click, pick a cheap Flash model:

  • The same website visits per email as a Pro model (x1.0), on the same clients in the same months
  • -54% cost per website visit with Flash
  • On positive replies, no model has enough of them yet to be ranked

The question

Every AI vendor sells a cheap, fast model (Flash) and an expensive, smarter one (Pro). For writing cold emails, the expensive one sounds like the safe bet. So which LLM is best for cold email?

We write cold emails for our clients with several models from Google, DeepSeek, Zhipu and others, and we track what each email gets. So we can answer with outcomes instead of opinions.

The trap in a model ranking

Ranking models straight off our data gives a winner. It is mostly a ranking of clients. A model that happened to write for clients whose prospects click a lot looks brilliant, whatever it wrote.

So we compared models like for like: a Flash model against a Pro model only on the same client in the same month, then pooled those match-ups. That keeps 90% of the emails with a link and cancels out who the client was and when the email went out.

The answer

Flash matched Pro on clicks, at half the cost. On the same clients in the same months, Flash models got 102 website visits for every 100 that Pro models got.

Each visit cost 46 with Flash for every 100 with Pro, because a Flash email costs about half as much to write and it brings the same clicks.

Replies: too rare to rank any model

Positive replies, a prospect answering with interest, are much rarer than clicks. Our Flash models produced too few of them for any comparison with Pro to mean something.

So we cannot tell you which model gets more positive replies, and we would distrust anyone who claims to from a handful of replies.

The rule this points to

When the email asks for a click, use Flash. The same visits for half the price leaves budget for more prospects.

When the email asks for a reply, test both on your own list. Nobody has the data to settle it yet, us included.

Why the cheap model holds up

Our reading: a cold email that asks for a click is short. It names a problem, offers a page and gets out of the way. A Pro model's extra reasoning has little to work with in a few short sentences, so the email it writes reads about the same to the prospect.

For the same reason, we put more effort into who receives the email and what it offers than into which model writes it.

Notes

  • On website visits, the Pro side is Gemini 3.1 Pro, the only Pro model that wrote emails with a link in this window.
  • This is not a split test: models were assigned per campaign, not per prospect. The like-for-like pooling (Mantel-Haenszel over client and month) removes the client mix, not every difference between campaigns.
  • Website visits per email, Flash against Pro: 1.02x (95% interval 0.79x to 1.31x). Cost per website visit: 0.46x (0.36x to 0.59x).
  • Holding the sequence step fixed as well, a Flash visit costs 0.58x a Pro visit (0.44x to 0.74x).
  • Each Flash model against Gemini 3.1 Pro, on the same clients in the same months:
    • Gemini 3 Flash: 1.00x the website visits per email (0.69x to 1.45x), a website visit at 0.40x the cost (0.27x to 0.57x); 130 website visits in 4,354 emails against 94 in 3,772.
    • Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite: 0.85x the website visits per email (0.58x to 1.26x), a website visit at 0.56x the cost (0.38x to 0.82x); 57 website visits in 3,239 emails against 71 in 3,367.
    • Gemini 3.5 Flash: 1.47x the website visits per email (0.96x to 2.25x), a website visit at 0.34x the cost (0.22x to 0.52x); 49 website visits in 1,438 emails against 54 in 2,288.
    • Gemini 3.6 Flash: 4 website visits in 268 emails against 59 in 2,622, too few for a ratio.
    • Gemini 3.7 Flash: 0 website visits in 86 emails against 57 in 2,842, too few for a ratio.
    • GLM 5.3 Flash: 0 website visits in 24 emails against 12 in 745, too few for a ratio.
    • DeepSeek V4 Flash: 0 website visits in 13 emails against 12 in 745, too few for a ratio.
  • Positive replies, Flash against Pro on the same clients and months: 1 in 8,196 emails against 22 in 40,201.

Method

Every sales cold email sent from April 15, 2026, read from our production database on October 2, 2026. Only people we started writing to at least 21 days before the read count, with every outcome they made since. A website visit counts only on emails that carry a link. On emails we send ourselves, link-scanner clicks (mail security bots such as Microsoft Safe Links) are removed; on emails sent through Instantly, the count is Instantly's. 4 positive replies come from the client's CRM. An email's cost is what the client paid for it. Our earlier article Flash or Pro LLM for cold email? ranked workflows without this like-for-like step. Scripts on GitHub.
