Key takeaway

For a cold email that asks for a click, pick a cheap Flash model:

  • The same website visits per email as a Pro model ({{x1 v.tier.rate.ratio}}), on the same clients in the same months
  • {{less v.tier.cost.ratio}} cost per website visit with Flash
  • On positive replies, no model has enough of them yet to be ranked

The question

Every AI vendor sells a cheap, fast model (Flash) and an expensive, smarter one (Pro). For writing cold emails, the expensive one sounds like the safe bet. So which LLM is best for cold email?

We write cold emails for our clients with several models from Google, DeepSeek, Zhipu and others, and we track what each email gets. So we can answer with outcomes instead of opinions.

The trap in a model ranking

Ranking models straight off our data gives a winner. It is mostly a ranking of clients. A model that happened to write for clients whose prospects click a lot looks brilliant, whatever it wrote.

So we compared models like for like: a Flash model against a Pro model only on the same client in the same month, then pooled those match-ups. That keeps {{share kept.emails kept.all}} of the emails with a link and cancels out who the client was and when the email went out.

The answer

Flash matched Pro on clicks, at half the cost. On the same clients in the same months, Flash models got {{index v.tier.rate.ratio}} website visits for every 100 that Pro models got.

{{chart visits}}

Each visit cost {{index v.tier.cost.ratio}} with Flash for every 100 with Pro, because a Flash email costs about half as much to write and it brings the same clicks.

{{chart cost}}

Replies: too rare to rank any model

Positive replies, a prospect answering with interest, are much rarer than clicks. Our Flash models produced too few of them for any comparison with Pro to mean something.

So we cannot tell you which model gets more positive replies, and we would distrust anyone who claims to from a handful of replies.

The rule this points to

When the email asks for a click, use Flash. The same visits for half the price leaves budget for more prospects.

When the email asks for a reply, test both on your own list. Nobody has the data to settle it yet, us included.

Why the cheap model holds up

Our reading: a cold email that asks for a click is short. It names a problem, offers a page and gets out of the way. A Pro model's extra reasoning has little to work with in a few short sentences, so the email it writes reads about the same to the prospect.

For the same reason, we put more effort into who receives the email and what it offers than into which model writes it.

Method

Every sales cold email sent from {{day windowFrom}}, read from our production database on {{day readOn}}. Only people we started writing to at least {{raw v.durationDays}} days before the read count, with every outcome they made since. A website visit counts only on emails that carry a link. On emails we send ourselves, link-scanner clicks (mail security bots such as Microsoft Safe Links) are removed; on emails sent through Instantly, the count is Instantly's. {{n crmRepliesAdded}} positive replies come from the client's CRM.
