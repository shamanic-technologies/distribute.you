Key takeaway

Turning off open tracking increases cold email performance:

  • {{lift replied}} replies
  • {{lift clicked}} clicks on a website link inside the email

The bet

Open tracking works by hiding a one-pixel image in every email. When the recipient's mail app loads it, the sending tool records an open. Many cold email tools offer it, and for months we had it on.

In late June 2026 we switched it off on new campaigns, during a deliverability cleanup. We kept the rest: same kind of offers, same sequence length, click tracking still on. Three months later we had enough sends on each side to ask a simple question. Did the emails without the pixel get more replies?

The data

The question is whether the pixel quietly sent emails to spam. Clicks and auto-replies rose with replies, and both need the email to reach an inbox: nobody clicks a link in a message they never saw, and an out-of-office usually fires only for mail that landed.

Why a pixel can cost you replies

Our reading, not something this data proves on its own. A tracking pixel is a remote image, served from a tracking domain that many other senders share. That is a classic signal spam filters look at, and a one-to-one email from a real person does not usually carry one.

The pixel also stopped telling us much. With it on, {{raw on.openPct}}% of people "opened". Apple Mail and some corporate security tools load every image before a human looks at the message, so a large part of those opens were machines. We were carrying a pixel for a number we could not act on.

The rule we ship now

No open tracking on cold email. Judge a campaign on replies and clicks.

A reply is a conversation starting, and a click is a person following a link. An open rate is neither.

What this data cannot tell you

  • This is not a controlled test. The two sides were sent in different months, and the switch came with other changes: new sending mailboxes, different lists (bounces went from {{pct bounced.on}} to {{pct bounced.off}}), and part of the later emails sent through our own pipeline. Any of these can move replies too. Read it as a direction, not a measured effect of the pixel alone.
  • Replies are rare: {{n replied.on.count}} and {{n replied.off.count}} of them. The gap is unlikely to be chance (two-proportion test, p = {{p replied}}; {{p repliedDelivered}} without bounces), but the exact size of the lift is uncertain.
  • Replies with interest are too few to say anything ({{n positive.on.count}} against {{n positive.off.count}}, p = {{p positive}}).

Method

Every cold email sequence created before {{day cutoff}} that sent at least one email, read from our production database on {{day readAt}}. The cutoff leaves every sequence at least two weeks to draw its replies. A sequence counts as "with the pixel" when the first configuration stored for its campaign had open tracking on, and "without" when it was off or when we sent it ourselves (those never carry a pixel). {{n total.unplaced}} sequences had no stored configuration and are left out. Only observed events count; an open we infer from a click is not an open. The query and the scripts that compute every figure are public on GitHub.
