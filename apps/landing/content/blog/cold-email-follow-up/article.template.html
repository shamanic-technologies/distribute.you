The question

Every cold emailer asks it: how many times do you follow up before you stop? Too few and you leave replies on the table. Too many and you annoy the people you hope to work with.

Our sequences send a first email and up to two follow-ups. So we measured what each follow-up adds on top of the emails before it, and set that against what it costs.

The answer

Two follow-ups doubled the positive replies. The first follow-up added {{raw lift1}}% to what the first email brought. The second added {{raw lift2}}% more.

{{chart all}}

A positive reply is a prospect answering with interest. Auto-replies, out-of-office messages and refusals do not count. Every step is measured on the same people, so each follow-up is judged on what it adds.

What a follow-up costs you

The useful comparison is what one more email costs against what it brings back. With us, a follow-up adds almost nothing to the bill: the three emails of a sequence are written in one go, and sending is not charged per email. If you send yourself, it is one more email from an inbox you already pay for.

Against that near-zero cost, the first follow-up brings {{raw lift1}}% more positive replies and the second {{raw lift2}}% more.

The rule this points to

Send the first email and two follow-ups. Each follow-up costs close to nothing and brings back people the first email missed.

The second follow-up adds less than the first. If you ever have to drop one, drop the second and keep the first.

Why the later emails still work

Our reading, not something this data proves on its own. A first email lands in a busy inbox and gets skipped, not refused. A short follow-up puts the thread back on top on a day the reader has a minute. Each round, the people most likely to answer already have, so the next email reaches a colder group and adds less.

What this data cannot tell you

  • Positive replies are rare, and the second follow-up's lift rests on a handful of them: read it as a direction, not a precise figure.
  • A reply counts for the last email sent before it. Someone who read the first email and answered after a follow-up arrived is counted for the follow-up.

  • Positive replies include the ones a client logged in their CRM (a phone call, a LinkedIn message), not only email answers.
