Key takeaway

Two follow-ups doubled the positive replies of our cold emails:

  • 18 positive replies from the first email, 37 after two follow-ups, from the same 32,166 people
  • $130 per positive reply on the first email, $181 on the first follow-up, $287 on the second

The question

Every cold emailer asks it: how many times do you follow up before you stop? Too few and you leave replies on the table. Too many and you pay for emails nobody wanted, and annoy the people you hope to work with.

We run cold email for our clients, and our sequences send a first email and up to two follow-ups. So we counted what each email in the sequence brings back, and what it cost.

The data

We looked at 91,000 cold emails sent to 32,000 people, from campaigns that ask for a reply. We counted positive replies only: a prospect answering with interest. Auto-replies, out-of-office messages and refusals do not count.

Every person is counted once, from the first email to the last, so each follow-up is judged on the same people.

The answer

Follow-ups doubled the positive replies. The first email alone brought 5.6 positive replies per 10,000 people. With two follow-ups, the same people reached 11.5.

Each email costs more per positive reply than the one before it: $130 for the first email, $181 for the first follow-up, $287 for the second.

The rule this points to

Send the first email and two follow-ups. Stopping after the first email gives up more than half of the positive replies.

The second follow-up is the most expensive email in the sequence, and it still brings about one positive reply in five (19%). Keep it if a new conversation is worth more to you than $287.

Why the later emails still work

Our reading, not something this data proves on its own. A first email lands in a busy inbox and gets skipped, not refused. A short follow-up puts the thread back on top on a day the reader has a minute. Each round, the people most likely to answer already have, so the next email reaches a colder group, and each reply costs more.

What this data cannot tell you

  • Positive replies are rare. The second follow-up carries 7 of them, so its price is a direction, not a precise figure.
  • A reply counts for the last email sent before it. Someone who read the first email and answered after a follow-up arrived is counted for the follow-up.
  • We almost never send a third follow-up (1 email in the window), so it is left out and this data says nothing about it.
  • Positive replies include the ones a client logged in their CRM (a phone call, a LinkedIn message), not only email answers.

Method

Every cold email sent from April 15, 2026 on campaigns that ask for a reply, read from our production database on October 1, 2026. Only people we started writing to at least 21 days before the read count, with every positive reply they sent since. Each email is filed under its place in the sequence.
