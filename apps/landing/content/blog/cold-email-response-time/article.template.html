The question founders ask on day 2

You launch a campaign on Monday. The first emails go out. On Tuesday morning you open your inbox, and it is quiet. Is the campaign working? Should you change the copy, the list, the offer?

That moment is where good campaigns get stopped too early. A quiet inbox on day 2 feels like an answer. In our data, it is a pause between two waves.

The first wave: the same day

At least {{raw floor}}% of first positive replies arrive within 24 hours of the first email. That is the finding of this study that holds as a conclusion. It also holds for each of our biggest clients taken alone.

Many of those come fast. Some prospects answer within the first hour, while your email still sits at the top of their inbox. When the first email reaches the right person with the right offer, that person often says yes on the spot.

Then the inbox goes quiet. In our data, the second day after the first email brought no new positive reply at all.

Then come the waves, after each follow-up

Our cold email workflows send two follow-ups: one on day 3 and one on day 10. Our early signal is that each follow-up brings its own wave of positive replies.

After the day-3 follow-up, a small wave arrives between day 3 and day 5. After the day-10 follow-up, a bigger one arrives between day 10 and day 14: about {{raw wave2PerDay}} positive replies a day, against about {{raw aroundPerDay}} a day in the days around it. We saw that second wave at several clients.

This is a signal, not yet a conclusion. The sample is small, and one client carries much of it. We will confirm it as more replies come in.

What this means for your campaign

Positive replies come in waves: the same day, then after each follow-up. So a cold email campaign is not judged at 48 hours. Here is the plan we follow for our clients:

  1. Read day 1 for the first wave. Good copy sent to the right people earns replies the same day.
  2. Let both follow-ups go out. A quiet day 2 is normal. Our follow-up study found that two follow-ups doubled the positive replies.
  3. Judge the campaign a few days after the day-10 follow-up. That is when the second wave lands.

Four questions founders ask us

Does a quiet day 2 mean the campaign failed? No. In our data, no positive reply landed on day 2, and the waves still came after it.

Why not judge on the first day alone? The first day is the biggest single day, and plenty of positive replies still come after it. Stopping early gives those up.

Do follow-ups annoy prospects? Our follow-ups are short and land in the same thread. In our data, the days after the day-10 follow-up look like one of the best moments of the sequence.

What about prospects who answer weeks later? They exist. Some of our positive replies came more than a month after the first email. Each lead keeps a next follow-up date, so a late yes still finds its way to you.
