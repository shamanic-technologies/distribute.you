Key takeaway

How a cold email agency charges decides who carries the risk, and what the agency is paid to optimise.

  • Retainer: you pay for effort, results are your risk.
  • Per meeting or per lead: the agency carries the risk, and gets paid to count, not to qualify.
  • Per contact or a funded budget: you pay for the work done, so ask to see what each reply cost.

Retainer: the classic, and the hardest to judge

A retainer buys you a team for the month: list building, copy, sending, reporting. It is simple to budget and the agency has room to do things properly. The catch is that a quiet month costs the same as a good one. The top Google result for this question is a Reddit thread asking whether a $5k monthly agency fee is worth it. The honest answer: only if you can see, every month, what came out of it.

Per meeting and per lead: the risk moves, and the incentive moves with it

Paying only when a meeting is booked sounds like the safest deal on the table. The agency eats the cost of the campaigns that miss. But it is now paid per calendar slot, not per buyer, so the definition of a "qualified" meeting becomes the whole contract. Per lead has the same shape with an even softer word: a lead can be a positive reply, a "send me more info", or a click.

Before signing either, get the definition in writing, and ask what happens to a meeting where the person never shows up or was never a buyer.

Per person contacted: you pay for the work, so the list is the question

This is the AiSDR model, and it is refreshingly plain. You know the price of each prospect reached before you start, and the vendor has no reason to inflate what a "lead" means. What it moves onto you is the targeting. As Alex Vacca put it on X: stop A/B testing emails nobody should have received. When you pay per person, the list is where your money goes, so ask how it is built and how often it is checked.

A funded budget: the ad account model

The last model works like Google Ads. You fund an amount, it becomes campaign credit, and the agency spends it on finding people, writing to them and following up. You do not pay per meeting and you do not pay for idle time. What makes it fair is the reporting: you should see, on your own account, what each positive reply cost you, per audience. Without that number, a budget is a retainer with extra steps.

Five questions to ask any cold email agency

  1. What exactly am I charged for? Time, meetings, leads, contacts or spend. One line, no asterisk.
  2. How do you define a lead or a qualified meeting? In writing.
  3. Whose domains send the emails? If they are yours, your reputation is on the line.
  4. Can I see what each positive reply cost me? Per audience, not as a monthly summary.
  5. How do I stop? Notice period, commitment length, what happens to the data.
