The question

A cold email is a cheap way to put a link in front of a buyer. Cheap per email. What nobody publishes is what it costs per click: the moment a stranger reads your email and decides to open your website. That number is what a paid channel is judged on, so it should be what cold email is judged on too.

The test

We sent 125,000 cold emails on real campaigns for real clients, across {{n best.workflowsPastFloor}} workflows we A/B tested past a thousand emails each: a workflow is one AI content template and one model. This article prices every one of them, the winners and the ones we retired. Our clients only ever get the winner, so two numbers matter: what a click cost across everything we tested, and what it costs on the workflow that won.

A click needs a link, so every price below is measured on the emails that carried one to the client's site. An email with no link cannot buy a click.

The rule

Why

A click is a small decision made in a few seconds. Everything that shortens those seconds lowers its price: a link to click, a short email, a short subject, a reader who is at their desk. And everything that makes the reader a decision-maker who does not own the company raises the odds: a director evaluates tools for a living, an owner of a three-person firm does not.

What it means if we run your campaigns

You do not set any of this by hand. Each campaign on distribute.you runs on the workflow with the best measured cost for what it is buying, across every client, and that pick moves as the data above moves. Method

  • Scope: sales cold email outreach only. Journalist outreach, PR pitches and every other channel we run are excluded.
  • Window: 15 April to 11 September 2026, every client account. Production data from the distribute.you platform, read on 12 September 2026.
  • Volume: {{n volume.emails}} emails to {{n volume.people}} people across {{n volume.orgs}} clients, {{usd volume.spend}} of platform spend. Of those, {{n volume.linked.emails}} emails to {{n volume.linked.people}} people carried a link to the client's site; they cost {{usd volume.linked.spend}} and {{n volume.linked.clicks}} people clicked. {{n volume.workflows}} workflows ran with tracked spend (a workflow is one AI content template and one model), {{n best.workflowsPastFloor}} of them past 1,000 emails; workflows with no tracked spend are excluded.
  • Click: the sending platform reported that the recipient clicked a link in the email, attributed to the email the platform named. Counted once per person, on the first click. Emails with no link in the body are excluded from every price, because a click needs a link to land on.
  • Link scanners: a corporate mail gateway fetches every URL in a message before the reader sees it, so a raw hit on a tracking link is not a person. On the emails we send ourselves, a hit is classified before it counts: a HEAD request, a known scanner user agent, a browser string carrying a full build number that no current browser reports, or the same reader fetching the unsubscribe link within a minute. There is no time threshold, because a person on a phone reads a message within seconds too. {{n scanner.demoted}} of {{n scanner.decided}} such hits were demoted. Clicks the sending provider reports on its own infrastructure are not affected, and they are most of this window: {{n volume.clicksBySource.provider}} of the {{n volume.clicks}} clicks counted here come from its tracking, {{n volume.clicksBySource.selfSend}} from our own redirect.
  • Pricing: every dollar here is what the client is charged, all in: lead sourcing, email verification, one email written for each recipient, the mailboxes and domains, sending, follow-ups, the team running it and our margin. There is no subscription and no retainer on top. That is what makes it comparable with a paid-media click and not comparable with a sending tool's monthly fee.
  • Cost per click: spend divided by clicks. Spend is everything the workflow cost before per-account discounts. Each email carries its own workflow's cost per email, so a bucket's spend is the sum over the emails in it.
  • Buckets: every bucket with at least one click is drawn and priced, with its emails and clicks printed under the bar so a reader can weigh it. One under {{n floors.minEmails}} emails or {{n floors.minClicks}} clicks is labelled (thin): the send volume is what makes a bucket unreadable, and a few hundred emails is a real send.
  • Tiers: Flash, the cheapest model of a family (Gemini 3 to 3.7 Flash, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, DeepSeek V4 Flash and GLM 5.3 Flash ran). Pro, the middle (Gemini 3.1 Pro, DeepSeek V4 Pro and GLM 5.2 to 5.3 ran). Frontier, the most expensive, which has not run in production beyond a handful of emails. The model is read per email from the generation record.
  • Length, paragraphs, subject, links: measured on the generated text of the email that was sent. A link is a URL or a bare domain in that text; the sending layer turns both into a tracked link.
  • Reader: country, seniority, company size and industry from the lead enrichment record. Local time from the reader's time zone where known.
  • Competitor figures: read from each company's own pricing page or benchmark report on the date beside it, quoted as published. We compute nothing from them.
  • Reproducing this: the queries and the derivation are committed at apps/landing/scripts/blog-data in our repository. Every figure on this page comes out of them.
  • Limits: not a randomised experiment. Our optimiser assigns workflows by measured cost, so the model tier, the length and the audience are correlated with each other. Each cut is reported alone; where a finding holds inside one tier we say so. {{n volume.linked.clicks}} clicks is a small count. We report counts and no confidence intervals.
