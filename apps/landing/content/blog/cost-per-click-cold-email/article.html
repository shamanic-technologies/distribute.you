The question

A cold email is a cheap way to put a link in front of a buyer. Cheap per email. What nobody publishes is what it costs per click: the moment a stranger reads your email and decides to open your website. That number is what a paid channel is judged on, so it should be what cold email is judged on too.

The test

We sent 120,000 cold emails on real campaigns for real clients, tracked every link, and divided what each campaign spent by the number of people who clicked. Then we cut the same data ten ways, to see what moves the price.

The answer

A click costs $11. About 6 people in 1,000 click. Between clients the spread is wide: the median client pays $7, the luckiest paid under $1, the least lucky paid $45.

The spread is the story. Below, in order of how much each one moves the price.

Put a link in the body

The single biggest lever. An email that carries a link in the text gets clicked 5x more often than one that does not, and the click costs $4 instead of $18. Obvious in hindsight. Seven in ten of our first emails did not do it.

The first email does the work

The first email buys a click for $5. The first follow-up buys one for $27. The second follow-up, $100. Follow-ups earn replies, not clicks: someone who wanted to look at your site did it the first time.

Shorter is cheaper

Under 500 characters, a click costs $2 to $3. Over 650, $13. The slope holds inside each model tier, so it is not the model: it is the reading time. One paragraph beats six, and a subject of 20 characters or fewer beats one over 40, by 4x.

Flash tier, again

Same finding as our Flash versus Pro study: the cheap tier writes the email that gets clicked. $3 a click with Flash, $20 with Pro. Part of that is the model writing longer. Part of it is the click rate itself: 12 per 1,000 against 4. The Chinese models we run, DeepSeek V4 Pro and GLM 5.3, have sent 3,759 emails and earned 5 clicks. Not enough to price yet.

Who you write to

A head of department, a VP or a director clicks for $3 to $5. A founder, $11. An owner or an entry-level contact, over $30. Company size runs the same way: a click at a 1 to 10 person company costs $24, at 50 people or more, $5 to $7. Both hold inside the Pro tier alone.

Industry moves the price by 10x. Financial services and IT click for $5 to $7. Medical practices, $58. A doctor reads email between patients, if at all.

Where they are

A European recipient clicks for $4, an Asian one for $4, a North American one for $14. France is the cheapest country we can price, $2; the United States the most expensive, $15. Most of our volume is American, so the $11 headline is mostly an American price. Europe stays 2 to 3x cheaper inside each model tier.

When it lands

Delivered between 2pm and 4pm in the recipient's own time zone, an email is clicked 17 times per 1,000 and a click costs $5. Delivered between 6am and 8am, 5 per 1,000 and $20. Tuesday and Wednesday are the cheapest days, $8 to $9; Thursday the dearest, $16.

The rule

Why

A click is a small decision made in a few seconds. Everything that shortens those seconds lowers its price: a link to click, a short email, a short subject, a reader who is at their desk in the afternoon. And everything that makes the reader a decision-maker who does not own the company raises the odds: a director evaluates tools for a living, an owner of a three-person firm does not.

Method

  • Scope: sales cold email outreach only. Journalist outreach, PR pitches and every other channel we run are excluded.
  • Window: April 15 to September 9, 2026, every client account. Production data from the distribute.you platform, read on September 9, 2026.
  • Volume: 120,652 emails to 35,589 people across 33 clients, $7,940 of platform spend, 748 people who clicked. 39 workflows (a workflow is one prompt and one model); workflows with no tracked spend are excluded.
  • Click: the sending platform reported that the recipient clicked a link in the email. Counted once per person, on the email they clicked first. Unsubscribe links do not count.
  • Cost per click: spend divided by clicks. Spend is everything the workflow cost before per-account discounts: lead sourcing, enrichment, sending and tokens. Each email carries its own workflow's cost per email, so a bucket's spend is the sum over the emails in it.
  • Buckets: a bucket is priced only with at least 2,000 emails and 20 clicks. Smaller ones are in the tables below without a price.
  • Tiers: Flash, the cheapest model of a family (Gemini 3 to 3.7 Flash and Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite ran). Pro, the middle (Gemini 3.1 Pro, DeepSeek V4 Pro and GLM 5.2 to 5.3 ran). The model is read per email from the generation record.
  • Length, paragraphs, subject, links: measured on the generated text of the email that was sent. A link is a URL in that text. Seven in ten first emails carry none, and some of those still record a click; we count the click as the platform reports it and do not know which link it was.
  • Recipient: country, seniority, company size and industry from the lead enrichment record. Local time from the recipient's time zone, known for 92% of emails.
  • Limits: not a randomised experiment. Our optimiser assigns workflows by measured cost, so the model tier, the length and the audience are correlated with each other. Each cut is reported alone; where a finding holds inside one tier we say so. 748 clicks is a small count. We report counts and no confidence intervals.
