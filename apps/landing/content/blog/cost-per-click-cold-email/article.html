The question

A cold email is a cheap way to put a link in front of a buyer. Cheap per email. What nobody publishes is what it costs per click: the moment a stranger reads your email and decides to open your website. That number is what a paid channel is judged on, so it should be what cold email is judged on too.

The test

We sent 125,000 cold emails on real campaigns for real clients, across 23 workflows we ran past a thousand emails each: a workflow is one AI content template and one model. Each workflow ran for its own clients, so this is not a split test: every finding below says whether it still holds when we compare on the same clients in the same months (a conclusion) or not (a signal).

A click needs a link, so every price below is measured on the emails that carried one to the client's site. An email with no link cannot buy a click.

The answer

A click costs $3 across every workflow we ran. We do not single out the cheapest workflow: picked out of 23, its price is partly luck.

Below, what moves the price, in order of how much.

The first email does the work

The first email buys a click for $2. The first follow-up buys one for $7, the second for $36. Follow-ups earn replies, not clicks: someone who wanted to look at your site did it the first time.

A conclusion: the first email stays the cheapest click when we compare on the same clients in the same months.

Shorter is cheaper

Under 400 characters, a click costs $1. Over 800 it costs $7. It is the reading time. Six paragraphs or more cost $7 a click against $2 for one, and a subject of 20 characters or fewer beats one over 40.

A signal, not a conclusion: on the same clients in the same months, the length gap is too small to rule out chance.

Flash tier, again

Same finding as our Flash versus Pro study: the cheap tier writes the email that gets clicked. $2 a click with Flash, $5 with Pro. Part of that is the model writing longer, and part of it is that more people click.

A conclusion: Flash stays the cheaper click when we compare the two tiers on the same clients in the same months.

Who you write to

A director, a VP or a manager clicks for $2 or less. A C-suite contact, $4. A founder, $6; an owner, $6. Company size runs the same way: a click at a company over 1,000 people costs $1, at a 1 to 10 person company $7.

Industry is the widest spread of any reader trait on this page. Research clicks for $1, hospitals for $3, IT for $4. Medical practices pay $8. A practitioner reads email between patients, if at all.

The seniority gap is a conclusion: it holds on the same clients in the same months. Company size and industry are signals we have not confirmed that way.

Where they are

A European or Asian reader clicks for $2, a North American one for $4. France is the cheapest country we price, $1; Canada is the dearest, $5, then the United States at $4. Most of our volume is American, so the $3 headline is mostly an American price.

A signal, not a conclusion: on the same clients in the same months, the gap between continents is too small to rule out chance.

When it lands

The afternoon and the small hours are the cheap windows, $2 a click between 2pm and 4pm in the reader's own time zone. The dear ones bracket the working day: $4 between 6am and 8am, $5 between 5pm and 7pm. Sunday is the cheapest day, $2; Thursday the dearest, $5.

A signal, not a conclusion: on the same clients in the same months, the gap between time windows is too small to rule out chance.

The rule

Why

A click is a small decision made in a few seconds. Everything that shortens those seconds lowers its price: a link to click, a short email, a short subject, a reader who is at their desk. And everything that makes the reader a decision-maker who does not own the company raises the odds: a director evaluates tools for a living, an owner of a three-person firm does not.

What it means if we run your campaigns

You do not set any of this by hand. Each campaign on distribute.you runs on the workflow with the best measured cost for what it is buying, across every client, and that pick moves as the data above moves. Method

  • Scope: sales cold email outreach only. Journalist outreach, PR pitches and every other channel we run are excluded.
  • Window: 15 April to 11 September 2026, every client account. Production data from the distribute.you platform, read on 2 October 2026, counting only the clicks and replies that had happened by 12 September 2026.
  • Emails too young to count: an email sent today has not had time to earn its click or its reply, so counting it makes every price read too high. We measured how long an outcome takes to arrive after the email that earned it: 95 in 100 website visits within 11 days, 95 in 100 positive replies within 9.6 days, measured on emails at least 45 days old so a slow one had time to show. The emails sent in the last 11 days of the window, from 1 September 2026 on (13,915 of them), are left out of every figure on this page, their clicks and replies with them.
  • Volume: 125,117 emails sent to 37,636 people across 36 clients. Every price and rate on this page is read on the 111,202 old enough to count, to 33,411 people across 34 clients: $6,683 of platform spend. Of those, 38,785 emails to 18,499 people carried a link to the client's site; they cost $2,104 and 623 people clicked. 51 workflows ran with tracked spend (a workflow is one AI content template and one model), 23 of them past 1,000 emails; workflows with no tracked spend are excluded.
  • Click: the sending platform reported that the recipient clicked a link in the email, attributed to the email the platform named. Counted once per person, on the first click. Emails with no link in the body are excluded from every price, because a click needs a link to land on.
  • Link scanners: a corporate mail gateway fetches every URL in a message before the reader sees it, so a raw hit on a tracking link is not a person. On the emails we send ourselves, a hit is classified before it counts: a HEAD request, a known scanner user agent, a browser string carrying a full build number that no current browser reports, or the same reader fetching the unsubscribe link within a minute. There is no time threshold, because a person on a phone reads a message within seconds too. 372 of 411 such hits were demoted. Clicks the sending provider reports on its own infrastructure are not affected, and they are most of this window: 690 of the 691 clicks counted here come from its tracking, 1 from our own redirect.
  • Pricing: every dollar here is what the client is charged, all in: lead sourcing, email verification, one email written for each recipient, the mailboxes and domains, sending, follow-ups, the team running it and our margin. There is no seat and no retainer on top. That is what makes it comparable with a paid-media click and not comparable with a sending tool's monthly fee.
  • Cost per click: spend divided by clicks. Spend is everything the workflow cost before per-account discounts. Each email carries its own workflow's cost per email, so a bucket's spend is the sum over the emails in it.
  • Buckets: every bucket with at least one click is drawn and priced, with its emails and clicks printed under the bar so a reader can weigh it. One under 100 emails or 2 clicks is labelled (thin): the send volume is what makes a bucket unreadable, and a few hundred emails is a real send.
  • Tiers: Flash, the cheapest model of a family (Gemini 3 to 3.7 Flash, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, DeepSeek V4 Flash and GLM 5.3 Flash ran). Pro, the middle (Gemini 3.1 Pro, DeepSeek V4 Pro and GLM 5.2 to 5.3 ran). Frontier, the most expensive, which has not run in production beyond a handful of emails. The model is read per email from the generation record.
  • Length, paragraphs, subject, links: measured on the generated text of the email that was sent. A link is a URL or a bare domain in that text; the sending layer turns both into a tracked link.
  • Reader: country, seniority, company size and industry from the lead enrichment record. Local time from the reader's time zone where known.
  • Competitor figures: read from each company's own pricing page or benchmark report on the date beside it, quoted as published. We compute nothing from them.
  • Reproducing this: the queries and the derivation are public on GitHub. Every figure on this page comes out of them.
  • Limits: not a randomised experiment. Our optimiser assigns workflows by measured cost, so the model tier, the length and the audience are correlated with each other. Each cut is reported alone; where a finding holds inside one tier we say so. 623 clicks is a small count. We report counts and no confidence intervals.
