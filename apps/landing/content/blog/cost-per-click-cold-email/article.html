The question

A cold email is a cheap way to put a link in front of a buyer. Cheap per email. What nobody publishes is what it costs per click: the moment a stranger reads your email and decides to open your website. That number is what a paid channel is judged on, so it should be what cold email is judged on too.

The test

We sent 125,000 cold emails on real campaigns for real clients, across 25 workflows we A/B tested past a thousand emails each: a workflow is one AI content template and one model. This article prices every one of them, the winners and the ones we retired. Our clients only ever get the winner, so two numbers matter: what a click cost across everything we tested, and what it costs on the workflow that won.

A click needs a link, so every price below is measured on the emails that carried one to the client's site. An email with no link cannot buy a click.

The answer

A click costs $4 across every workflow we tested. The workflow that won the A/B tests buys one for $1, and it runs on the Flash tier, Gemini 3.5 Flash.

Below, what moves the price, in order of how much.

The first email does the work

The first email buys a click for $2. The first follow-up buys one for $7, the second for $29. Follow-ups earn replies, not clicks: someone who wanted to look at your site did it the first time.

Shorter is cheaper

Under 400 characters, a click costs $1. Over 800 it costs $7, on too few clicks to be a solid read. It is the reading time. Six paragraphs or more cost $7 a click against $2 for one, and a subject of 20 characters or fewer beats one over 40.

Flash tier, again

Same finding as our Flash versus Pro study: the cheap tier writes the email that gets clicked. $2 a click with Flash, $6 with Pro. Part of that is the model writing longer, and part of it is that more people click.

Who you write to

A director, a VP or a manager clicks for $2 or less. A C-suite contact, $5. A founder or an owner, $6 to $7. Company size runs the same way: a click at a company over 1,000 people costs $1, at a 1 to 10 person company $7.

Industry is the widest spread of any reader trait on this page. Research clicks for $1, hospitals and IT for $4. Medical practices pay $10. A practitioner reads email between patients, if at all.

Where they are

A European or Asian reader clicks for $2, a North American one for $4. France is the cheapest country we can price, $1; the United States the most expensive, $5. Most of our volume is American, so the $4 headline is mostly an American price.

When it lands

The afternoon and the small hours are the cheap windows, $2 a click between 2pm and 4pm in the reader's own time zone. The dear one is the morning rush: $5 between 6am and 8am. Wednesday is the cheapest day, $3; Thursday the dearest, $5.

The rule

Why

A click is a small decision made in a few seconds. Everything that shortens those seconds lowers its price: a link to click, a short email, a short subject, a reader who is at their desk. And everything that makes the reader a decision-maker who does not own the company raises the odds: a director evaluates tools for a living, an owner of a three-person firm does not.

What it means if we run your campaigns

You do not set any of this by hand. Each campaign on distribute.you runs on the workflow with the best measured cost for what it is buying, across every client, and that pick moves as the data above moves. Today that is a click for $1. You pay the budget the campaign spent, nothing else, and the cost of each click lands on your dashboard.

Method

  • Scope: sales cold email outreach only. Journalist outreach, PR pitches and every other channel we run are excluded.
  • Window: 15 April to 11 September 2026, every client account. Production data from the distribute.you platform, read on 11 September 2026.
  • Volume: 124,460 emails to 37,338 people across 34 clients, $8,412 of platform spend. Of those, 42,325 emails to 20,483 people carried a link to the client's site; they cost $2,522 and 677 people clicked. 59 workflows ran with tracked spend (a workflow is one AI content template and one model), 25 of them past 1,000 emails; workflows with no tracked spend are excluded.
  • Click: the sending platform reported that the recipient clicked a link in the email, attributed to the email the platform named. Counted once per person, on the first click. Emails with no link in the body are excluded from every price, because a click needs a link to land on.
  • Link scanners: a corporate mail gateway fetches every URL in a message before the reader sees it, so a raw hit on a tracking link is not a person. On the emails we send ourselves, a hit is classified before it counts: a HEAD request, a known scanner user agent, a browser string carrying a full build number that no current browser reports, or the same reader fetching the unsubscribe link within a minute. There is no time threshold, because a person on a phone reads a message within seconds too. 271 of 309 such hits were demoted. Clicks the sending provider reports on its own infrastructure are not affected.
  • Pricing: every dollar here is what the client is charged, all in: lead sourcing, email verification, one email written for each recipient, the mailboxes and domains, sending, follow-ups, the team running it and our margin. There is no subscription and no retainer on top. That is what makes it comparable with a paid-media click and not comparable with a sending tool's monthly fee.
  • Cost per click: spend divided by clicks. Spend is everything the workflow cost before per-account discounts. Each email carries its own workflow's cost per email, so a bucket's spend is the sum over the emails in it.
  • Buckets: a bucket is a solid read past at least 1,000 emails and 20 clicks. Every bucket with at least one click is still drawn and priced, with its emails and clicks printed under the bar so a reader can weigh it; one under either floor is labelled (thin).
  • Tiers: Flash, the cheapest model of a family (Gemini 3 to 3.7 Flash, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, DeepSeek V4 Flash and GLM 5.3 Flash ran). Pro, the middle (Gemini 3.1 Pro, DeepSeek V4 Pro and GLM 5.2 to 5.3 ran). Frontier, the most expensive, which has not run in production beyond a handful of emails. The model is read per email from the generation record.
  • Length, paragraphs, subject, links: measured on the generated text of the email that was sent. A link is a URL or a bare domain in that text; the sending layer turns both into a tracked link.
  • Reader: country, seniority, company size and industry from the lead enrichment record. Local time from the reader's time zone where known.
  • Competitor figures: read from each company's own pricing page or benchmark report on the date beside it, quoted as published. We compute nothing from them.
  • Reproducing this: the queries and the derivation are committed at apps/landing/scripts/blog-data in our repository. Every figure on this page comes out of them.
  • Limits: not a randomised experiment. Our optimiser assigns workflows by measured cost, so the model tier, the length and the audience are correlated with each other. Each cut is reported alone; where a finding holds inside one tier we say so. 677 clicks is a small count. We report counts and no confidence intervals.
