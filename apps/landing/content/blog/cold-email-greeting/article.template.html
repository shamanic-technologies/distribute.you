Key takeaway

Skip the greeting when your cold email asks for a click:

  • {{lift v.none.rate v.name.rate}} website visits with no greeting, against the first name alone
  • On positive replies, the opening made no measurable difference

The question

Every cold email opens somehow. "Hi Marie," is the polite default. "Marie," alone is shorter and reads like a note from a colleague. Some emails skip both and open on the point.

We write cold emails for our clients, and until recently the model writing them picked the opening on its own. So all three openings are in our data. We counted which one works better for the two things a first email can ask for: a click, or a reply.

The data

We looked at {{round total.emails}} cold emails from two kinds of campaign. One asks the prospect to visit a website and carries a link: {{round v.totals.emails}} emails. The other asks for a positive reply, a prospect answering with interest: {{round r.totals.emails}} emails. Auto-replies, out-of-office messages and refusals do not count as positive replies.

Each email is filed under how the first email of its sequence opened, so a follow-up's result counts for the opening the prospect saw first.

The answer

Skipping the greeting got more clicks. When the email asks for a website visit, {{pct v.none.rate}} of emails with no greeting got one, against {{pct v.name.rate}} with the first name alone and {{pct v.greet.rate}} with "Hi" and the first name. That is {{lift v.none.rate v.name.rate}} visits against "Marie,", and the gap holds when we compare the two openings on the same clients in the same months. Against the full "Hi Marie," it reads {{lift v.none.rate v.greet.rate}}, but that gap mostly disappears on the same clients: a signal, not a conclusion.

{{chart visit}}

Replies: the opening made no measurable difference

When the email asks for a reply, the three openings landed too close to rank. Positive replies are rare, and the gap between the openings is smaller than what chance alone moves at this volume.

So when you want a reply, stop agonizing over "Hi".

The rule this points to

Want the click? Skip the hello. Open on the point and put the link in front of the reader sooner.

Want a reply? Open however reads natural to you. Our data shows no opening winning there.

Why skipping the hello helps the click

Our reading: a website visit is a one-click decision. An email that gets straight to the point puts the offer and the link in front of the reader sooner, and a salutation is one more line before it.
