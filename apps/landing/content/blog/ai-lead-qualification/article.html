We bring our clients signups and booked meetings, done for you, and they report a 2.0x median ROI. It starts with emailing the right people. A good lead often shows on its website: a phone number missing from the header, no reviews on the first screen, no way to book. So an AI looks at each prospect's homepage and answers our clients' own yes/no questions about it. Which AI should do that, and at what price? We tested six on our real data.

The test: six AI models, 79 homepages

We took every homepage screenshot our lead checks had stored: 79 companies our clients prospect, from roofers to dental clinics and recruiting firms. We asked each screenshot 5 questions our clients care about:

  • Is there no phone number in the header?
  • Is there no customer review rating on the first screen?
  • Is there no live chat widget?
  • Does the first screen show a street address?
  • Is there a clear call-to-action button?

Six models answered every question on the same screenshots: Jev 1.13 by TypeSafe, GPT-6 Luna from OpenAI, Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite and Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite from Google, Claude Haiku 5.5 and Claude Sonnet 5.5 from Anthropic. Where they disagreed, we looked at the page ourselves.

Accuracy: 96 to 99 right answers in 100, for every model

All six read a homepage right 96 to 99 times in 100. Compared answer by answer with Jev, no model was measurably better or worse. The misses were small details, like a cookie-settings icon read as a chat bubble, or a street address in small print under a map.

Price: Jev is the cheapest

Jev checks 1,000 websites for $0.35. Claude Sonnet 5.5 costs $13.19 for the same 1,000 checks, with no measurable gain in accuracy. Jev is built to judge rather than to write: you give it the screenshot and the question, and it answers yes or no with how sure it is. No text to generate, so the answer costs input only.

How we use Jev to qualify leads

  1. You describe a good lead in plain words, down to what you would notice on their website. One of our clients sells to roofers and asked: "Is there no clearly visible phone number in the header?"
  2. We take a screenshot of each prospect's homepage.
  3. Jev answers the question on the screenshot, before we pay for anyone's email. Leads that fit get your emails.

The questions we get

"Wouldn't a bigger model do better?" Claude Sonnet 5.5, the largest model we tested, scored within chance of Jev at 38x the price.

"What about cookie banners?" Jev first answers whether the screenshot really shows the homepage. A page hidden behind a banner, or an error page, is marked unreadable and not counted as a lead.

"How sure is each answer?" Jev returns a probability, not just yes or no, so you choose how sure it must be before a lead counts.

"Can I ask anything?" Anything visible on the first screen of the homepage. Questions about text deeper in the site read the page text instead.

  • Benchmark: 79 homepage screenshots (first screen) stored by our lead qualification checks for our clients' prospects, 5 questions each, 395 checks per model, run October 21, 2026 through our model router. Same screenshot bytes for every model.
  • Right answers: 378 checks scored. Where all six models gave the same answer (285 checks) that answer is taken as right; every other check (93) was settled by looking at the screenshot. On a page covered by a cookie wall, and on a hosting error page, "unreadable" and the visible answer both count as right. 17 checks we could not settle by eye (a quote pop-up that may be a chat, a parked domain, borderline buttons) are left out.
  • Scores (right of 378, 95% Wilson interval): Jev 1.13 373 (96.9% to 99.4%); GPT-6 Luna 373 (96.9% to 99.4%); Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite 375 (97.7% to 99.7%); Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite 374 (97.3% to 99.6%); Claude Haiku 5.5 366 (94.5% to 98.2%; 19 checks came back empty twice and count as wrong); Claude Sonnet 5.5 377 (98.5% to 100.0%).
  • Each model against Jev, paired on the same checks (exact sign test, Bonferroni over 5): GPT-6 Luna p 1.00; Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite p 0.69; Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite p 1.00; Claude Haiku 5.5 p 0.12; Claude Sonnet 5.5 p 0.22. None differs from Jev beyond chance.
  • Price: what each model's run was billed at our catalogue prices, divided by its 395 checks, times 1,000. Cloudflare Clef and Clef-flash were in the plan but not scored: our Cloudflare account's free daily quota ran out during the run.
