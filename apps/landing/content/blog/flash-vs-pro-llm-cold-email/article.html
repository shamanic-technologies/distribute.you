The question

Every LLM vendor now sells the same family in two tiers. A cheap one (Gemini Flash, DeepSeek V4 Flash, GLM Flash, Kimi K2.6) and an expensive one (Gemini Pro, DeepSeek V4 Pro, GLM 5.3, Kimi K3). The expensive tier costs roughly 5 to 20 times more per token.

So which one should write your cold emails? Pay 7x more per email and convert better, or pay 7x less and convert worse? We sent 95,661 cold emails written by both tiers, on real campaigns for real clients, and measured what each dollar bought.

The answer is simpler than expected

  • If you want website visits (a click on the link in the email): the cheap tier wins. It bought a visit for 3.24 USD. The expensive tier paid 17.83 USD.
  • If you want positive replies (someone writes back to know more or to book a meeting): the expensive tier wins, and it is not close. It produced 6 positive replies per 1,000 USD. The cheap tier produced fewer than 1.

Put the two charts side by side and the rule writes itself.

Why the two tiers buy different things

We do not have a controlled explanation and we will not invent one. The pattern is consistent with a simple mechanism: a cheap model writes a serviceable email around a link, and enough people click links. A strong model writes an email a buyer actually answers, and answering is a much higher bar than clicking. Across the whole dataset a positive reply was 22 times rarer than a visit before any model choice.

The premium is real. The expensive model's tokens were about a third of everything our largest workflow spent, against a tenth for a cheap-model workflow. On the reply side that premium came back many times over. On the click side it did not come back at all.

Method

Everything below is production data from the distribute.you platform, read on September 8, 2026.

  • Scope: sales cold email outreach only. Journalist outreach, PR pitches and every other channel we run are excluded.
  • Window: April 14 to September 8, 2026, every client account.
  • Volume: 95,661 emails to 33,243 people, 6,715.53 USD of platform spend, 698 website visits, 31 positive replies. Produced by 20 workflows (a workflow is one prompt and one model). Workflows configured but never run are excluded.
  • Spend: everything the workflow cost, before per-account discounts: lead sourcing, enrichment, sending and tokens. Not tokens alone.
  • Website visit: a recipient clicked the tracked link. Counted once per person.
  • Positive reply: a recipient wrote back with interest (a question, a request for details, a meeting). Classified automatically, reviewed by a person on the account. Negative replies, out-of-office and unsubscribes do not count.
  • Cheap tier: Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite (Gemini 3 Flash Preview before July 24) and Gemini 3.5 to 3.8 Flash. 4 workflows, 34,506 emails, 1,368.69 USD.
  • Expensive tier: Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview. 12 workflows, 57,650 emails, 4,868.84 USD.
  • Not yet measurable: DeepSeek V4 Pro and GLM 5.3 have 3,505 emails and 478 USD between them, too little to rank. DeepSeek V4 Flash, GLM-5.3-Flash and Kimi have no production spend yet. We will update this page when they do.
  • Template check: the two tiers did not run the same prompts evenly. Restricted to the prompt family that asks for a click, the cheap tier still bought the visit for less (3.24 vs 8.06 USD) and the expensive tier still produced six times the positive replies on the same spend.
  • What this is not: a randomised experiment. Our optimiser assigns workflows to campaigns by measured cost, so expensive workflows ran more on reply-seeking campaigns. And 31 positive replies is a small count. We report counts and no confidence intervals.

What this means if we run your campaigns

You never pick a model. Each campaign on distribute.you is served by the workflow with the best measured cost for what the campaign is buying, across every client, and the pick changes when the numbers change. The charts above are the same numbers our optimiser reads. You pay the budget your campaign spent and nothing else, and you watch the cost of each positive reply land on your dashboard.
