An observational study on production data. 20 cold email workflows, 6,715.53 USD of platform spend, April 14 to September 8, 2026. No inferential statistics: the counts are small and we report them as counts, so you can judge them yourself.

Summary

  • Flash-tier models are the cheapest way to get a website visit. The four Flash workflows bought 423 visits for 1,368.69 USD, a cost per visit of 3.24 USD. The twelve Pro workflows bought 273 visits for 4,868.84 USD, 17.83 USD each.
  • Pro-tier models are the only tier that produced positive replies in volume. Pro workflows produced 29 positive replies (168 USD each). Flash workflows produced 1 positive reply on 1,368.69 USD of spend.
  • The pattern holds inside the same prompt template family. Restricted to the link-driven template family, Flash still wins the visit (3.24 vs 8.06 USD) and Pro still wins the reply (6 replies vs 1 on comparable spend).
  • Chinese Pro tiers are not yet measurable. DeepSeek V4 Pro and GLM 5.3 have 478 USD of spend and 1 positive reply between them. Chinese Flash tiers have not been run in production at all. We will update this page when they have.
  • The generating model is a large share of what an email costs. Pro tokens were 32 percent of the total spend of our largest Pro workflow; Flash-Lite tokens were 7 to 10 percent of a Flash workflow's spend. Pro pays more per email and still buys the outcome that matters.

Why this question matters

Every LLM vendor now ships two tiers of the same family. Google has Flash and Pro. DeepSeek has V4 Flash and V4 Pro. Z.ai has GLM-5.3-Flash and GLM-5.3. Moonshot has Kimi K2.6 and K3. The Flash tier is five to twenty times cheaper per token. The question a cold email operator has to answer is not which model writes a nicer paragraph. It is which tier produces a reply from a buyer at the lowest cost, once the whole pipeline is paid for: the lead, the enrichment, the sending infrastructure and the tokens.

We run cold email campaigns for our clients on our own sending domains, and we try several model and prompt combinations against the same audiences. Each combination is a workflow. Every dollar a workflow spends and every outcome it produces is recorded per workflow. That gives us a dataset that a single company running a single model cannot have.

Method

Population

Every cold email workflow that produced billable spend on the distribute.you platform between April 14, 2026 and September 8, 2026, across every client account. 20 workflows qualify. Workflows that were configured but never spent (60 of them, mostly the newer Chinese-model variants) are excluded because they have no outcomes to measure.

Definitions

  • Spend: the platform cost attributed to the workflow, summed over its lifetime, before any per-account discount. It includes lead sourcing, enrichment, sending and LLM tokens for every step of the workflow, not only the email generation step.
  • Website visit: a recipient clicked the tracked link in an email produced by the workflow. Counted once per recipient.
  • Positive reply: a recipient answered and the answer was classified as interest in the offer (a question, a request for details or a meeting). Automated classification, reviewed by a person on the account. We call this a sales interest on our dashboards. Negative replies, out-of-office and unsubscribes are excluded.
  • Cost per website visit (CPWV): spend divided by website visits.
  • Cost per positive reply (CPPR): spend divided by positive replies. Left blank when a workflow has fewer than two replies, because dividing by one is a coin toss, not a price.

Interpretation

The two tiers appear to optimise different steps of the funnel. A cheaper model writes a serviceable email with a link, and enough recipients click it to make the visit cheap. A stronger model writes an email that a buyer answers. We do not know from this data why. Plausible mechanisms include better use of the enrichment context (the Pro prompt receives the same company fields), a more specific first sentence, or a lower rate of the small factual slips that make a reader close the email. We have not run a blind rating of the emails and we do not claim one of these.

What we can say is operational. If the campaign's goal is a website visit (a signup funnel, a self-serve product), the Flash tier is the rational default and the data does not show a reply penalty large enough to matter, because nobody was expecting replies from those campaigns. If the goal is a conversation that leads to a meeting, the data does not support running Flash: one reply in 1,369 USD is not a price, it is an absence.

Limitations

  • Small counts. 31 positive replies in total, 19 of them from one workflow. A single additional reply moves a Flash workflow's CPPR from undefined to a number. We report counts and refuse to report confidence intervals on them.
  • Observational, not randomised. Workflows were assigned to campaigns by our own optimiser, which prefers whichever workflow is cheapest for the campaign's goal. Pro workflows therefore ran more often on reply-seeking campaigns and Flash workflows on visit-seeking ones. The link-driven-only comparison limits this, it does not remove it.
  • Moving targets. The Flash alias changed vendor model once and the Flash mid tier changed three times during the window. Gemini 3.1 Pro is a preview model. Prices quoted are today's list prices; DeepSeek moved to peak and off-peak pricing on August 16, 2026.
  • One channel, our pipeline. All emails were sent from our own domains through our own infrastructure, with our prompts. A different lead source or a different prompt could change the numbers.
  • Chinese vendors are early. 478 USD of spend on DeepSeek V4 Pro and GLM is enough to say they have not yet produced replies in volume, not enough to rank them. Their Flash tiers have no production spend. We will update this page as they accumulate.
  • Spend is gross. Some accounts carry a usage discount. Cost per outcome is computed on the gross figure so that workflows can be compared on the same basis.

How we run this at distribute.you

Every client campaign on distribute.you is served by the workflow with the best measured cost for the campaign's goal, across all clients, and the client pays the budget the campaign spent and nothing else. The tables above are the same numbers our optimiser reads. A client never has to pick a model; the point of measuring it is that we pick it for them, and change the pick when the numbers change. You can watch the cost of your own positive replies on your dashboard as they land.

If you want to see what a positive reply currently costs for your kind of buyer, start with your website URL. The first 30 USD are on us.

Questions people ask

Is Gemini Flash good enough for cold email?

For campaigns whose goal is a click, yes on this data: it bought visits at 3.24 USD against 17.83 USD for Pro. For campaigns whose goal is a reply, no on this data: one positive reply on 1,368.69 USD of spend.

Is DeepSeek cheaper than Gemini for cold email?

Per token, DeepSeek V4 Pro lists at roughly a third of Gemini 3.1 Pro. Per outcome we cannot say yet: three DeepSeek V4 Pro workflows have 430.51 USD of spend and one positive reply between them.

Why is cost per reply so much higher than cost per visit?

A visit is a click; a positive reply is a person typing an answer that says they are interested. Across the whole dataset there were 698 visits and 31 positive replies, so a reply is about 22 times rarer than a visit before any model choice.

Will you publish updated numbers?

Yes. The dataset grows every day and the Chinese-vendor rows are the ones we most want to fill. This page carries the date of its last data pull.
